## Executive decision

**Recommend Option C: a new greenfield application, built as a modular monolith with an explicit transactional core, preserving v0.8.1 as the protected reference.** Reuse verified rules and acceptance scenarios, not the old storage/dispatch architecture by obligation. This changes my provisional Round 2 recommendation. No production migration is needed, the owner's priorities favor long-term engineering quality, and the now-known employee, manual-appointment and native-chat requirements affect the same identities, transaction boundaries and ownership model that require replacement for reliable dispatch/recovery. Selective replacement of almost all those layers would create a temporary second architecture without a clear compensating benefit.

Claude has satisfactorily **responded** to all fifteen challenges, but has not **proven** all remedies. Review classification: **3 resolved, 5 conditionally resolved, 5 unresolved, 2 owner decisions required**. These labels distinguish design corrections from tested capabilities. There is no remaining need for another architecture debate; the next work, after approval, is a bounded implementation/proof program with code-review gates.

**Accept the combined safety direction only with the amendments below. Do not accept SC-1–SC-17 verbatim or claim commercial readiness.** Principal outstanding issues are execution before archive durability, recoverable identity completeness, precise withdrawal locking, old-dispatcher credential fencing, HA durability, and late submissions from a paused process. The Calendar-wide capacity-one rule is superseded for vNext by employee-level protection on a business-owned shared Calendar.

## 4. Material corrections before effect-capable implementation

### 4.1 Acceptance, archive and processing must share an admission contract

Counterexample: database commit exposes STOP/booking input; worker processes it; archive write fails; HTTP returns non-200; then database is lost. ACK discipline alone did not prevent an effect without recoverable evidence. Choose one protocol, including an execution-eligible seal for inputs/commands whose required independent evidence is durable. Handle archive-written/database-absent and database-written/archive-absent explicitly; do not compensate by deleting evidence. Grants and credential/configuration changes must not authorize downstream dispatch before their required evidence is sealed; withdrawal may suppress immediately even while acknowledgment is blocked. Conditional immutable writes need content hashes and stable names, including whole-batch versus per-event identity and conflicting repeats.

A per-channel sequence and daily manifest can reveal some interior gaps; they do not automatically prove completeness of an unrecorded final tail or survive sequence reuse after restore. Define authoritative high-water/seal records, key versions, durability/failure domains and a method to tell unknown completeness from verified completeness. If unable to prove completeness, retain suppression and quarantine. Raw full-body WORM storage is not a functional requirement: minimize independent recovery evidence where it still preserves identity, consent and dispatch obligations.

### 4.2 Identity derivation is not evidence reconstruction

R1 Q6's MAC inputs include offer ID and effect ordinal. A lost random offer or changed reducer/version/context can change those inputs. Maintain original action/provenance and identity derivation version/key references independently or use a durable identity ledger. A stable ID helps find the *original* event; it cannot prove no effect occurred or authorize recreating missing work. Recovery must never use newly computed availability or a different random employee assignment to invent the old intent. Replay stays inert until facts and ambiguity are resolved.

### 4.3 A freeze can outlive the reminder deadline

An absolute HTTP timeout bounds an invocation after it starts, not a process frozen before the socket call. A five-minute token cache lifetime does not expire a copied valid access token at Meta/Google. Even with one nontransferable attempt, an old owner could send long after R03's allowance or a withdrawal. Recheck local deadlines/versions immediately before submission; invalidation wins when observed before the dispatch transition. Define enforceable process/egress containment and an explicit residual threat envelope. Do not claim a universal deadline guarantee under arbitrary process suspension. This is a commercial activation gate, not permission to weaken R03 silently.

### 4.4 Withdrawal and admission need a real shared lock

Use a stable business/sender permission head (or an equivalent correctly keyed transactional lock) that both ingest suppression and arming must acquire. Lock order and supported transaction isolation are part of the contract. The head must exist without registering an unknown customer. Serialize against the same version; do not rely on `FOR SHARE` returning zero rows. Define how a suppress-only recognition disagreement is reviewed without auto-regranting permission. Preserve exact baseline withdrawal priority/phrases initially.

### 4.5 Failover and ETag experiments are not defaults

A synchronous commit is useful only with a documented promotion set/durability mode and old-primary fencing. Cluster epochs and code version gates protect local admissions, not already-fetched credentials. Degraded failover uses disaster recovery, with safe reads and new writes separated. R1 Q3's proposed ETag bump is **deferred**: 412 proves the precondition lost, not that the original operation won; another writer may have changed it. The bump itself can time out and must not create a second mutation authority or silently alter owner data. Read-only reconciliation remains the implementation default.

## 5. Final architecture, including the owner additions

- **Shape:** strict TypeScript/Node LTS modular monolith. Separate ingress, worker/dispatcher and private operator entry points from one versioned artifact. PostgreSQL is operational truth; Google remains actual appointment existence/time/version authority. Framework/query libraries are implementation choices, not safety proofs.
- **Foundation:** business-owned Calendar mapping, explicit employees/resources, customer identity and verified contact aliases, conversation participant/automation ownership, origin-aware appointments/adoption identities, schedule/resource revisions, durable operations/attempts/guards, permission head/evidence, scoped notifications/review and recovery evidence seals.
- **Execution:** local transactions admit effects, employee reservations and one unresolved event mutator; nontransferable armed sends/writes, full ownership checks, evidence-based finalization, durable reply/reminder state. No new distributed service/broker is justified yet.
- **Calendar:** one Calendar per business; multiple resource appointments may overlap on it. Appointments from unknown/manual events require event-aware assignment/conflict rules. No global Calendar lock as the capacity model. Manual external conflicts become Needs Attention, never automatic cancellation.
- **Channel:** preserve a parity behavior profile initially; future assistant-add-on profile explicitly separates customer messages, business-owner echoes, human ownership and intro epochs. Coexistence is a provider capability to establish before promising native App operation.
- **Manual/consent:** detect candidates, verify identity association and origin, use existing effective business-specific permission only after identity/appointment eligibility is proven. Otherwise pending authorization and owner attention; no unsolicited permission request. Customer-initiated Accept/Decline flow is future feature scope.
- **Recovery/security:** layered tenant scope/FKs/RLS, attributable operator grants, credential AAD and independent recovery custody; safe read-only investigation while dispatch is quarantined, suppression on incomplete evidence, offsite tested recovery under approved policy. No claim of external exactly-once execution.

## 6. Accepted decisions, rejected claims and remaining risks

**Accepted:** no same-ID retry assumption; nontransferable submission direction; single unresolved event mutation; retained current/historical identity; barrier on unknown output; full local authority; unfiltered sync plus preflight; real tenant/operator security; inert restore; observability and equivalent proof obligations for all implementation options.

**Rejected as defaults:** Calendar-wide capacity one for a multi-employee business; timeout-driven uncertainty release; ambiguous generic confirmation; ETag-bump recovery as established proof; five-minute cache as a credential fence; archive success after processing as adequate durability; deterministic IDs as complete evidence; RLS as authentication; numeric workload/retention/SLO or pricing approval inferred from an architect's proposal.

**Still critical:** provider contracts and coexistence eligibility; deployed lock/claim/HA proofs; recovery-ledger completeness; consent/manual identity integrity; staff assignment under external edits; future timezone policy; privacy/custody; operator staffing/cost of blocked work. These are bounded work-package gates in 12, not invitations to restart the design debate. No original report is rewritten and no implementation begins in this assignment.
