## TTL recommendation and boundaries

| Template | Recommended maximum custom TTL | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `nukmiris_seven_day_v1` | 900 seconds / 15 minutes | Avoid days-late reminders and bound the provider queue |
| `nukmiris_confirmation_v1` | 900 seconds / 15 minutes | Keep the request close to its 24-hour schedule; do not let it remain pending through later reminders |
| `nukmiris_followup_v1` | 900 seconds / 15 minutes | Keep the follow-up close to its intended window, only after the original was delivered |
| `nukmiris_final_v1` | 300 seconds / 5 minutes | Minimize stale two-hour reminders |

S2 says the Utility default is **30 days**, custom Utility TTL is **30–43,200 seconds**, and `-1` restores 30 days. The app intentionally rejects that long-lived sentinel for reminders. A shorter legal TTL is accepted; an absent, cleared or longer TTL cannot pass activation/job checks. Automatic recategorization to Marketing or a cleared TTL also stops sending.

The existing 15-minute local allowance controls **when NukMiris may submit** a due reminder. Provider TTL controls retry delivery **after acceptance**. For a final reminder submitted 15 minutes late, the five-minute provider budget can extend delivery to roughly 20 minutes after its original due time, still well before the appointment. This does not claim a strict 15-minute device-delivery deadline. If a future interval override cannot fit the maximum TTL plus the 15-second transport timeout before start, the job is skipped.

TTL does not retract already delivered text, stop someone reading an old message, or cancel a message accepted before the correction. Existing v0.8.0 messages accepted under the previous long TTL may still surface. Appointment/version/quote checks therefore remain necessary even after TTL is configured. Uncertain sends are retained for reconciliation, never blindly repeated because a receipt is missing. Receipt latency also means local absence of delivery proof is not conclusive proof of nondelivery.

S2 contains two internal inconsistencies: it labels its template endpoint placeholder `PHONE_NUMBER_ID`, although S1 and the official v26 WABA schema locate template assets under the WABA; its Marketing example uses 120 seconds despite the same table's 12-hour minimum for Marketing. This implementation exports **Utility** definitions and uses the WABA-based lookup confirmed by S1/S5. It does not automatically create/update any Meta template or copy the inconsistent Marketing example.

## Permission assessment and recording

The existing customer-initiated booking proves the WhatsApp identity and lets the app handle that conversation. It does **not** record evidence that the customer opted into subsequent appointment communications from the named business. Neither the outgoing “Te enviaremos…” sentence nor a customer's attendance “Sí” is treated as consent. Current stored booking data alone cannot establish the permission required by S3.

S3 allows general business communication permission; a separate WhatsApp-specific checkbox or additional booking step is **not required by that excerpt**. Existing website, paper, phone or other qualifying consent can be used if its actual scope includes these appointment communications, the business was named, and applicable law is satisfied. The owner must retain the source evidence. Code cannot verify that a claimed verbal/form permission was genuinely obtained.

The new `reminder_permission_events` table records business ID, existing WhatsApp identity, customer ID where known, grant/withdrawal, actual occurrence time, recording time, source, exact wording, evidence reference and who recorded it. The application appends events rather than overwriting the earlier evidence. Current permission is determined by actual event time, with withdrawal winning a same-time tie; importing an older grant cannot undo a later opt-out. A grant requires an existing scoped customer. An unknown sender's opt-out can be retained without registering a customer. Evidence references are idempotent and conflicting reuse is rejected.

Ordinary booking, management and attendance replies keep working without a reminder grant. Proactive jobs with missing/withdrawn permission are skipped and the success message does not promise reminders. Dry-run identifies permission gaps without sending. Supported withdrawal messages include `STOP`, `No quiero recordatorios` and `No me envíes recordatorios`. Human chat remains open and stores the withdrawal message. Other in-person/phone/email requests must be honored through the local permission command; no claim of arbitrary-language opt-out understanding is made.

From `backend/` in PowerShell, using actual evidence and the stored identity shown by the scoped report:

```powershell
npm.cmd run reminders:permission -- --business-id BUSINESS_ID --whatsapp-id STORED_WHATSAPP_ID --grant --occurred-at "ACTUAL_ISO_TIME_WITH_OFFSET" --source "ACTUAL_SOURCE" --wording "ACTUAL_CUSTOMER_PERMISSION_WORDING" --evidence "ACTUAL_EVIDENCE_REFERENCE" --recorded-by "RESPONSIBLE_OWNER"
```

Use `--revoke` with the actual withdrawal evidence/time to record an external opt-out. These commands record evidence only: they do not collect consent, enable sending, modify appointments or send WhatsApp messages. General permission must actually cover the intended appointment communications; do not manufacture a record from an ordinary booking or silently bulk-grant existing customers.

## Required owner/Meta actions before production activation

1. Keep `NUKMIRIS_REMINDERS_MODE=off` during the update. Stop Node and repeat `npm.cmd run reminders:prepare -- --business-id BUSINESS_ID` for existing v0.8.0 installations. Its backup precedes the additive permission-table migration; existing activation time and data are preserved. No permissions are backfilled.
2. Configure each business's correct WABA ID and sending-number mapping. Its token must be authorized to inspect that account's templates/phone numbers and to send for that number. Shared development credentials still do not constitute completed independent business-owner onboarding.
3. Create/update the four accepted templates' TTL metadata in Meta through a verified supported account/template management operation. Preserve the accepted names/text/variables/buttons, and obtain/retain `APPROVED` Utility status for the exact language. No such external changes are performed by this code.
4. Run `npm.cmd run reminders:templates -- --business-id BUSINESS_ID --check`. It verifies sender membership, approval/category/language/content and returned TTL, and reports quality. Check every configured business rather than relying on one business's approval. A missing TTL response or unavailable status fails closed.
5. Record genuine qualifying customer permission, including a usable withdrawal method in the owner's consent/customer information process. The existing reminder messages are unchanged; no extra WhatsApp booking question is added.
6. Test in reminder `dry-run` first. Ordinary booking/management remains active; dry-run suppresses proactive reminder sends and Calendar attendance writes. Then the owner may explicitly choose `live` for designated recipients and verify real Meta template acceptance, TTL metadata, button callback payloads, delivered/read statuses, quoted replies, opt-out and Google attendance updates.

No automatic activation is included. Unknown Meta outcomes, paused/rejected templates, missing permissions and mismatched WABA/TTL are never bypassed by switching to free-form proactive messages. Template availability is read at startup and per job, rather than relying on a positive status cache or assumed delivery order. Status may still change between a read and POST; Meta remains the final send authority. An opt-out or Calendar move after an external send is already in flight cannot retract that submission.
